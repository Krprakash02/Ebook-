# Introduction

## You're Probably Making More Security Mistakes Than You Think

Take a quick look at your phone.

Is the screen lock something a classmate could guess? Is your email password also your Wi-Fi password, or the same one you used for that shopping site three years ago? Have you ever tapped a link in a message because it said a parcel was waiting?

If you said yes to any of these, you are in the overwhelming company of ordinary, sensible people.

This is not a book about geniuses in dark rooms. Most attacks that harm ordinary people do not begin with clever technical wizardry. They begin with a normal moment: a rushed afternoon, a convincing message, a free download, a call that sounds official. Attackers know that people are busy and trusting, so they design their tricks around that.

That is the idea behind this book. Not "you are careless," but "you are human, and some habits quietly make you easier to target."

### What this book is (and isn't)

It is fifty short chapters, each about one everyday mistake. Every chapter tells you:

- what the habit looks like,
- why it feels reasonable,
- what can go wrong,
- how an attacker might take advantage (at a high level, never as instructions),
- what to look out for,
- how to protect yourself, and
- what to do if you have already made the mistake.

It is *not* a promise that you will never be scammed. No one can promise that. It is also not a fear campaign. One risky habit does not mean disaster is inevitable. The goal is to shift the odds in your favour with small, realistic changes.

### Who it's for

You do not need any technical background. When a technical word is unavoidable, it is explained straight away. The examples come from daily life in India and elsewhere: UPI, OTPs, WhatsApp, fake customer-care numbers, job offers that seem too good to be true, and so on. Many of the lessons apply anywhere.

### How to use it

- **Read straight through**, or jump to the chapters that sound like you. Each one stands alone.
- **Do the 30-Second Security Fix** at the end of each chapter. Small actions add up.
- **Use "If You've Already Made This Mistake"** without guilt. Acting quickly matters far more than feeling embarrassed.
- **Finish with the self-assessment and the seven-day plan** at the back.

### A note on honesty

Some examples in this book are invented, and they are always labelled **Hypothetical Example**. Real incidents are labelled **Documented Incident** and come with a reference. Statistics are used only when they come from a source you can check. Where experts or sources disagree, the book says so.

Cybersecurity changes quickly. Apps update, rules change, and scammers invent new stories. Where a chapter points to an official website, helpline, or setting, please confirm it is still current before relying on it.

### One last thing

If you have ever been scammed, you were not stupid. You were targeted by someone whose job was to be convincing. Throughout this book, the blame belongs to the people who commit fraud, never to the people they deceive.

Let's begin.
